# Pillar Content

> Hub-and-cluster authority · OmniSkill Social crew

One comprehensive pillar page on a broad topic, surrounded by cluster articles covering sub-topics in depth, all deliberately interlinked.

## Why the structure works

Three effects compound:

1. **Demonstrated coverage.** A site with a pillar and fifteen supporting pieces on a subject visibly knows that subject better than a site with one post.
2. **Internal link equity.** Clusters link to the pillar, concentrating authority where it competes for the hardest term.
3. **Query coverage.** The pillar catches broad head terms; clusters catch specific long-tail ones. Together they cover a topic's full search surface.

The structure is the point. A pillar page with no clusters is just a long article; clusters with no pillar are scattered posts.

## 1. Choose the pillar topic

It must be:
- **Broad enough** to support 10+ genuine sub-topics
- **Narrow enough** to actually cover comprehensively
- **Commercially relevant** — connected to what the business sells
- **Winnable** at current domain authority

"Marketing" is too broad. "Email subject lines" is too narrow. "Email marketing for e-commerce" is a pillar.

Source topics from `/omniskill:content-strategy` rather than brainstorming.

## 2. Build the pillar page

**Comprehensive, not exhausting.** Cover every major sub-topic well enough to be genuinely useful standalone, then link out to the cluster piece for depth. The pillar is a map with real terrain, not a table of contents.

Structure:
- Clear definition and scope up front — answer the head query in the first paragraph
- A section per major sub-topic, each self-contained and useful
- A contextual link from each section to its cluster article
- Table of contents with anchor links for anything long
- Original data, frameworks, or visuals that exist nowhere else

Length follows coverage, not a target. Typically 2,000–5,000 words, but a 2,000-word pillar that covers the topic beats a padded 6,000-word one.

**Make it linkable.** Original research, a useful framework, or a genuinely definitive resource earns external links. Pillars are the pages worth building links to.

## 3. Build the clusters

Each cluster article:
- Covers **one** sub-topic in real depth
- Stands alone as useful — never a stub existing only to link
- Links **back to the pillar** with descriptive anchor text
- Links **sideways to sibling clusters** where genuinely relevant
- Targets a specific long-tail query

Aim for 8–15 clusters per pillar. Publish the pillar first, then clusters steadily.

## 4. Get the internal linking right

This is where most implementations fail.

- **Every cluster links up to the pillar.** Non-negotiable — this is the mechanism.
- **The pillar links down to every cluster.** Update it as each publishes; a pillar that never gets updated leaves clusters orphaned.
- **Clusters link to relevant siblings**, not to all of them indiscriminately.
- **Descriptive anchor text** — the target's topic, not "click here" and not the same exact-match phrase every time.
- **Contextual placement** in the body, which carries more weight than a footer link block.

## 5. Maintain

Pillars decay. Review quarterly: refresh statistics and dates, add clusters as new sub-topics emerge, update links, consolidate clusters that have started competing with each other for the same query.

A well-maintained pillar compounds for years. An abandoned one slides steadily.

## 6. Measure at cluster level

Judge the cluster as a unit, not article by article. Track combined organic traffic, pillar ranking for the head term, cluster rankings for long-tail, internal link flow, and conversions.

Expect 3–6 months before the structure shows its effect. Judging at week four kills strategies that were working.

## Output

Pillar outline with sections, the cluster list with target queries, the internal linking map, the publication sequence, and the maintenance schedule.

## Checks before delivering

- Pillar is broad enough for 10+ sub-topics and narrow enough to cover.
- Every cluster is independently useful.
- Every cluster links to the pillar; the pillar links to every cluster.
- Anchor text is descriptive and varied.
- Something on the pillar is genuinely original.
- Maintenance cadence is scheduled.
