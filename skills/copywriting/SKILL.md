# Copywriting

> Rewrites any page copy · OmniSkill Social crew

## 1. Diagnose before rewriting

Read the existing copy and answer three questions from the visitor's position:

1. **What is this?** If it takes more than five seconds, the headline has failed.
2. **Is it for me?** Copy addressing everyone addresses no one.
3. **What do I do next?** One obvious action, or the page ends in a shrug.

Most weak copy fails the first question while being confident about the third.

## 2. Gather the raw material

The best copy is discovered, not invented. Before writing, collect:

- **Customer language** — reviews, support tickets, sales-call notes, community threads. Use their words, not internal vocabulary. Customers say "it keeps breaking"; the team says "reliability concerns".
- **Proof** — numbers, outcomes, named customers, guarantees.
- **Objections** — what makes people hesitate. Each unanswered objection is a silent exit.
- **The actual differentiator** — what is genuinely true here and not of competitors.

Copy without this is guessing in a confident voice.

## 3. The rewrite

**Headline.** State the outcome for a specific person. Clear beats clever, every time. If it could sit on a competitor's site unchanged, it says nothing.

**Subhead.** How it works, or who it is for. Earns the next scroll.

**Body.** Benefit first, feature as evidence. Not "AI-powered reconciliation engine" but "Close the books in a day instead of a week — automatic reconciliation matches transactions for you."

**Proof near every claim.** A claim without adjacent evidence is decoration.

**Objection handling.** Address the top three directly. A short FAQ beats pretending they do not exist.

**One call to action.** Specific language, repeated at natural decision points. Not five competing buttons.

## 4. Line-level rules

- **Cut every word doing no work.** First drafts are typically 30% longer than they need to be.
- **Active voice.** "We reduced onboarding to 10 minutes", not "onboarding was reduced".
- **Second person.** "You" far more than "we". Visitors care about their problem.
- **Specific numbers.** "10,000 requests/month" not "generous limits". Specificity reads as truth because it is checkable.
- **Short sentences.** Vary rhythm, but default short.
- **No hedging.** "May help you potentially reduce" says nothing. Claim it or cut it.
- **Kill the clichés** — "seamless", "robust", "game-changing", "revolutionary", "world-class". They are noise that signals nobody thought about the sentence.

## 5. Structure for scanning

Nobody reads. They scan, then read the part that caught them.

Descriptive subheads that tell the story alone. Short paragraphs. Bold the load-bearing phrase, not random words. Bullets for lists, prose for arguments. The key message visible without scrolling.

## 6. Substantiate

Every claim must be true and supportable. Performance numbers need a source; comparative claims need evidence; testimonials need permission and must be real. Route anything that sounds like a regulated claim to `/omniskill:legal-risk`.

## 7. Verify

Read it aloud — awkward writing is audible before it is visible. Check it against the three diagnostic questions. Confirm nothing was over-trimmed into vagueness; brevity is not the goal, clarity is.

## Output

The rewritten copy, plus a short note on what each change fixes. Deliver alongside the original so the difference is reviewable.

## Checks before delivering

- Headline answers "what is this" in five seconds.
- Every claim has adjacent proof.
- Top three objections addressed.
- One primary call to action.
- No hedging, no clichés, no unsupported superlatives.
