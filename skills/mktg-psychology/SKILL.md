# Marketing Psychology

> Behavioral triggers · OmniSkill Marketing crew

A multiplier on the other Marketing skills, not a standalone deliverable. Layer it onto copy, pricing, and flows the rest of the crew produces.

## The line that governs everything here

**Use these to help people decide, not to trick them into deciding.**

The test: would the customer, told exactly what was done and why, feel respected or manipulated? Real scarcity is information. Invented scarcity is a lie that happens to convert — and it converts once, then costs the relationship, the refund, and increasingly the regulatory action.

Everything below is legitimate when the underlying fact is true. Nearly every one has a fraudulent twin. The dark-patterns section is not optional.

## The principles that actually move behaviour

**Loss aversion.** Losses weigh roughly twice gains. "Stop losing 6 hours a week" outperforms "Save 6 hours a week" on identical facts. Frame around what is currently being lost.

**Anchoring.** The first number sets the reference. Show the enterprise tier before the standard one; state annual value before monthly price. Anchor against the cost of the problem, not just competitor pricing.

**Social proof.** People follow similar people. Specificity and similarity beat volume — "4,200 agencies" beats "thousands of customers", and a testimonial from a matching role beats a famous logo.

**Authority.** Credentials, published data, and named expertise reduce perceived risk. Earned authority only; borrowed authority collapses on inspection.

**Commitment and consistency.** Small initial actions raise follow-through. Progressive forms and free tiers work partly through this — which is also why a first step must be genuinely small.

**Reciprocity.** Genuine value given first creates willingness to reciprocate. A real tool or real analysis works; a gated PDF does not.

**Scarcity and urgency.** Powerful and the most abused. Only use when true: real inventory limits, real cohort dates, a real deadline. A countdown that resets on refresh is fraud, and increasingly an enforcement target.

**Cognitive fluency.** Easy-to-process claims feel truer. Short sentences, plain words, clean layout, high contrast. Complexity reads as risk.

**Paradox of choice.** More options lower conversion past a point. Three tiers, a recommended default, one primary CTA.

**Peak-end rule.** People remember the peak and the end. Invest in the onboarding "aha" moment and the final confirmation screen disproportionately.

**Endowment.** Ownership raises value. Free trials with real data imported, configured workspaces, and personalisation all work through this.

## Applying it

**To copy** — lead with what is being lost, anchor against the cost of the problem, place specific and similar social proof near the decision point, and cut every sentence that raises processing cost.

**To pricing** — anchor high, three tiers, mark the recommended one, show annual savings against monthly. See `/omniskill:pricing`.

**To flows** — small first commitment, visible progress, defaults set to the option that genuinely suits most users, friction removed before the decision and never added after it.

## Dark patterns — do not build these

These are prohibited regardless of measured lift. Several are illegal in the EU, UK, and a growing list of US states, and enforcement is active.

- Fake countdowns, fake stock levels, fake "3 people viewing this"
- Fabricated or incentivised-but-undisclosed reviews
- Confirmshaming ("No thanks, I like wasting money")
- Roach motel: easy to subscribe, hard to cancel
- Pre-ticked consent or opt-in boxes
- Hidden costs revealed only at the final step
- Disguised ads, or a "close" button that opens a link
- Obstructed unsubscribe

If a request is for one of these, say plainly that it is not something to build, and offer the legitimate version that achieves the same goal. There almost always is one: real deadlines instead of fake ones, genuinely popular defaults instead of pre-ticked boxes, honest total pricing instead of drip pricing.

Route anything uncertain to `/omniskill:legal-risk`.

## Checks before delivering

- Every scarcity or urgency claim is factually true.
- Social proof is real, specific, and disclosed where incentivised.
- Nothing on the dark-patterns list is present.
- Consent is opt-in and unticked.
- Total cost is visible before the final step.
- Cancelling is as easy as subscribing.
