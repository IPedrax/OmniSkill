# NDA Triage

> Auto-triage NDAs · OmniSkill Legal crew

**This is analysis, not legal advice.** No attorney-client relationship exists. Escalate anything non-standard to a qualified attorney.

Most NDAs are routine and should take minutes. The purpose of triage is to clear the standard ones quickly and catch the few that are not.

## The eight checks

**1 — Mutual or one-way?**
Mutual is standard when both sides will share. A one-way NDA where we are the only discloser is fine; one where we are the only *recipient* puts all the obligation on us. Flag it.

**2 — Definition of confidential information**
Reasonable: information marked confidential, or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential given the circumstances.
Overbroad: *all* information exchanged, regardless of marking or nature. That makes every email a compliance risk.
Check the standard exclusions are present — already public, independently developed, rightfully received from a third party, required by law.

**3 — Term and survival**
Confidentiality obligations of 2–5 years are standard. Perpetual obligations on ordinary business information are not, and are hard to comply with in practice.
**Trade secrets are the exception** — perpetual protection for genuine trade secrets is normal and acceptable.
Note the difference between the agreement's term and how long the obligation survives it. These are separate clocks and are often confused.

**4 — Permitted disclosure**
Must allow disclosure to employees, advisors, and affiliates who need to know and are bound by equivalent obligations. An NDA that forbids telling your own lawyer is unworkable.
Must allow legally compelled disclosure, ideally with notice to the discloser where lawful.

**5 — Residuals**
A residuals clause lets the recipient use what people remember without notes. It substantially weakens protection.
Acceptable when we are primarily the *recipient*. Dangerous when we are the discloser sharing real technical detail. Which side benefits determines whether it is fine or fatal.

**6 — No obligations beyond confidentiality**
An NDA should do one thing. Watch for smuggled terms: non-solicit, non-compete, IP assignment, exclusivity, licence grants. These belong in a negotiated commercial agreement, not a routine NDA. Their presence upgrades this from triage to `/omniskill:contract-review`.

**7 — Return or destruction**
Standard. Confirm there is an exception for backup and archival copies and for retention required by law — otherwise it is a promise that cannot be kept.

**8 — Governing law and venue**
Home jurisdiction or a neutral major one is fine. An unfamiliar or foreign venue for a routine NDA is disproportionate and worth pushing back on.

## Verdict

| Verdict | When |
|---|---|
| **Accept** | Mutual, reasonable definition, 2–5 year term, standard exclusions, no smuggled obligations. Sign it. |
| **Negotiate** | One or two off-market terms with everything else standard. Propose specific replacement language. |
| **Escalate** | Non-compete or IP assignment present, perpetual obligations on ordinary information, uncapped indemnity, or the deal is high-value. Send to an attorney. |

## Output

Lead with the verdict. Then a short table: each of the eight checks, its status, and a one-line note. Then proposed language for anything being negotiated.

Keep it to one page. An NDA review that runs longer than the NDA has lost the plot — if it genuinely needs that depth, the verdict was **escalate**.

## Checks before delivering

- All eight checks addressed.
- Verdict stated first, not buried.
- Which side we are on is identified, since it changes several answers.
- The not-legal-advice scope note is present.
