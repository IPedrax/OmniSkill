# Ad Creative

> Scales ad variations · OmniSkill Marketing crew

Producing enough creative to test properly, structured so the results mean something.

## The premise

Creative is the largest lever in modern paid media. Targeting is increasingly automated; creative is what still separates campaigns. Volume matters because most variations lose — and the winners are rarely the ones predicted in the meeting.

## 1. Vary along axes, not at random

Fifty random variations teach nothing. Fifty structured ones identify what works.

**Axis 1 — Angle** (the argument). Usually the biggest performance driver.
- Problem/agitation · Outcome/aspiration · Social proof · Authority · Comparison · Novelty · Objection handling · Risk reversal

**Axis 2 — Hook** (the first 3 seconds or first line).
- Question · Bold claim · Statistic · Pattern interrupt · Direct address to the segment · Story open · Negative framing

**Axis 3 — Format**.
- Static image · Carousel · UGC-style video · Talking head · Screen recording/demo · Text-heavy · Meme

**Axis 4 — Proof**.
- Testimonial · Data · Demo · Before/after · Case study · Logos

Build a matrix and sample from it deliberately. Cover the angle axis broadly first — angle beats hook, and hook beats format, for how much performance moves.

## 2. Write the copy

**Primary text** — first line must earn the expand. Front-load the hook; everything after the fold is for people already interested.

**Headline** — short, benefit-led, readable at a glance.

**CTA** — specific action, matching what actually happens next. A "Shop now" that opens a lead form is a bounce.

Rules that hold across platforms:
- Specific beats clever. "Cut invoice processing from 3 days to 20 minutes" outperforms wordplay.
- Speak to one person, in their words. Mine reviews, support tickets, and community threads for actual phrasing.
- Lead with the outcome, not the mechanism. Buyers want the result; the feature is evidence.
- One idea per ad. An ad arguing three things argues none.

## 3. Respect the format

Design for sound-off and thumb-speed. Most impressions are silent and scrolled past.

- Hook in the first 1–2 seconds of video. There is no second chance.
- Burn in captions.
- Correct aspect ratio per placement — 9:16 for stories and reels, 1:1 or 4:5 for feed.
- Legible at phone size: large text, high contrast, minimal words on image.
- Brand early enough to register, not so early it reads as an ad before the hook lands.

## 4. Comply

Ad platforms reject and sometimes penalise accounts for policy violations. Before shipping:

- Claims must be substantiable. Health, financial, and income claims are heavily restricted.
- No before/after imagery in restricted categories.
- No implying knowledge of personal attributes ("Struggling with debt?" as direct address is a common rejection).
- Required disclosures present.

Substantiate claims before publication, not after a challenge. Route anything borderline to `/omniskill:legal-risk`.

## 5. Test and read results

- Launch enough variations to find a winner, but give each enough budget to exit the learning phase. Twenty ads on a small budget produce twenty inconclusive results.
- Judge on the business metric — cost per acquisition, not click-through. High CTR with poor conversion usually means the ad promised something the page does not deliver.
- Refresh on fatigue: rising frequency with falling CTR means the audience has seen it.
- Log why each winner won, by axis. That compounds into a creative strategy; a folder of winning files does not.

## Output

The variation matrix, full copy for each variation, format and placement notes, and the test plan with budget and duration.

## Checks before delivering

- Variations differ along named axes, not arbitrarily.
- Every claim is substantiable.
- Designed for sound-off, phone-sized viewing.
- Success metric is a business metric.
- Enough budget per variation to reach a readable result.
