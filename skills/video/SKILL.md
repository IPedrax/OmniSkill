# Video

> Scripts + produces video · OmniSkill Social crew

## The mechanic that decides everything

**Retention drives distribution.** Every platform promotes video that holds attention and buries video that does not. This makes structure more important than production value — a phone-shot video with a strong hook outperforms a polished one that opens slowly.

## Short-form (under 60s)

**Structure**

| Beat | Timing | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2s | Stop the scroll. Everything depends on this. |
| Setup | 2–5s | Frame the problem or promise |
| Payoff | 5–45s | Deliver, in steps or beats |
| Close | last 5s | Loop, or a single clear next step |

**Hook patterns that work:** the result first ("This cut our invoice time from 3 days to 20 minutes"), a contradiction ("Stop doing X"), a direct question to a segment, a visual pattern interrupt, or opening mid-action.

**Never open with** a logo, an introduction, or "hey guys". Those are retention cliffs.

**Rules**
- Cut every pause. Short-form has no room for breathing space.
- New visual information every 2–3 seconds — cut, zoom, text change, or motion.
- Text overlay always; most viewing is silent.
- Loop the ending back to the opening where it fits — it lifts replays, which count.
- One idea per video. A second idea belongs in a second video.

## Long-form

**Structure:** hook and promise (0–30s) → context → the substance in chapters → payoff → next step.

Title and thumbnail do most of the work on discovery. Write them *first* — if a compelling title cannot be written, the video concept is weak and no editing rescues it.

Deliver on the title's promise early. Withholding the answer to stretch watch time is the most reliable way to lose an audience permanently.

Chapter markers, visible structure, and a real conclusion.

## Writing the script

Write **spoken**, not written. Read every line aloud; anything that trips is rewritten. Contractions, short sentences, direct address.

Script the hook word for word. Everything after can be bulleted if the delivery is natural, but the first two seconds are too important to improvise.

Cut ruthlessly: a 90-second script usually has 60 seconds of content in it.

## Production

**Shot list** before filming: each shot, its purpose, and duration. Filming without one produces footage that will not cut together.

**Baseline quality that actually matters** — good audio (far more than good video), a light source in front of the subject, a stable shot, and correct aspect ratio per platform. Beyond that, production value has sharply diminishing returns against structure.

**Aspect ratios:** 9:16 for Reels, Shorts, TikTok. 16:9 for YouTube long-form. 1:1 or 4:5 for feed.

## Accessibility

- **Burned-in captions** for short-form — most viewers watch silent, and platform auto-captions are unreliable.
- **Uploaded caption files** for long-form, so they are searchable and correctable.
- Describe important visual information aloud where the audio alone would not convey it.
- Sufficient contrast on text overlays.
- Avoid rapid flashing — it is a genuine seizure risk, not a style preference.

## Repurpose deliberately

One long-form video yields short clips, an audio version, a written article, and a set of social posts. Plan this at scripting time — extracting clips afterwards from footage not shot for it produces weak clips.

Re-cut per platform rather than reposting one file everywhere. See `/omniskill:social`.

## Output

Full script with the hook written verbatim, shot list, caption text, title and thumbnail concepts, and per-platform delivery specs.

## Checks before delivering

- Hook lands within 2 seconds and is scripted word for word.
- One idea per video.
- Works with sound off.
- Captions specified.
- Title and thumbnail written before production.
- Aspect ratio correct per target platform.
